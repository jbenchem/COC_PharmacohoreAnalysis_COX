## ⚠️ Provenance warning — read before quoting any number

These numbers come from **three different code versions** over one day. Before
anything goes in the document, do one clean run and re-record.

| numbers | produced with | status |
|---|---|---|
| curation, confound, excess SD | X = 2,265 columns | stable, unaffected by later changes |
| potency 5×5, classifier, CMFOA screen | X = 2,264 (Ipc dropped) | **superseded** — see below |
| anything after | X = 2,263 (`NumUnspecifiedAtomStereoCenters` also dropped) | not yet run |

Three changes landed after the runs below and will move the numbers slightly:

1. **`NumUnspecifiedAtomStereoCenters` dropped** from the descriptor block. It is
   a curation artefact, not chemistry — the same molecule scores 2 drawn without
   stereo and 0 drawn with it, so it encodes how completely a record was
   annotated. ChEMBL medicinal chemistry specifies stereochemistry and
   natural-product databases often do not, so leaving it in lets the model learn
   which database a compound came from.
2. **A 5-NN Tanimoto null added.** Predicts each test compound as the mean
   potency of its 5 most similar training compounds. It is harder than the other
   three nulls, so **the margin will drop** — that is the test getting honest, not
   the model getting worse.
3. **Contamination matching now uses the InChIKey skeleton as well as the full
   key.** The full key includes the stereo layer, so the same substance drawn
   without stereochemistry got a different key and slipped past the check.

**Control 2 also has a known flaw, unfixed at time of writing.** It pools
confirmed inactives across *all* assay formats while everything else in the
pipeline is single-format. It reports 821 inactives where the BAO_0000357 set
holds 610. The consequence is measurable: the potency model scores **0.599 on
format-pooled inactives and 0.749 on format-filtered ones**. Do not publish
0.599.

### Stereochemistry — ChEMBL's field is curation metadata, not chemistry

ChEMBL left `chirality` unpopulated for **7,561 of 9,137 compounds (83%)**.
Derived from the structures instead:

| derived status | n |
|---|---:|
| achiral | 5,493 |
| unspecified | 1,345 |
| structure_only | 706 |
| no_structure | 17 |

5,493 of those have **no stereocentres at all**. Filing them as "unknown
stereochemistry" would describe ChEMBL's curation backlog rather than the
chemistry. Audit trail in `stereo_status_derived.csv` — one row per compound
whose status was assigned here, with the observation that justifies it. Keep it
under version control; it is the difference between the dataset as ChEMBL reports
it and the dataset as modelled.

### Featurisation

2,048 Morgan/ECFP4 bits (radius 2, **chirality off**) + 217 RDKit descriptors.

- Chirality off was decided by measurement: only **10 of 4,402** curated COX-2
  compounds carry a ChEMBL-confirmed single-stereoisomer status.
- 11 descriptor values exceeded float32 range and were clipped (`Ipc`, dropped).
- 12 non-finite, 11 zero-variance descriptors — reported, imputed inside folds,
  never dropped across the whole dataset.
- Fingerprint density 45.7 bits/molecule, 21 dead bits.
- **5 round-trip parse failures**: CHEMBL3355602, 3355603, 3355604, 3355611,
  3355612. Consecutive IDs, so almost certainly one paper. RDKit wrote these
  structures during standardisation and cannot read them back — a round-trip
  failure means the stored structure is not what was standardised. Belongs in the
  curation log rather than silently absent.

## Finding 1 — assay formats are chemically confounded

**Contribution #1. The strongest result here.**

### The confound

Can a scaffold-grouped classifier recover which assay format a compound was
measured in, from structure alone?

```
real AUC   0.887 ± 0.070
null AUC   0.489 ± 0.013      (labels shuffled — lands on chance, as it must)
```

Whole chemotype families were run in one assay and not another. Assay format is
not scattered across chemical space; it is clustered in it.

### The paired deltas — 223 cross-format compounds

Read the **excess SD**: `√(observed SD² − 0.68²)`, the divergence beyond
measurement noise. A constant offset is correctable and a model can learn it. The
excess is what no calibration removes.

| transition | median | SD | excess SD | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 221 − 219 | −0.860 | 0.615 | **0.00** | constant offset — one correction fixes it |
| 357 − 221 | +0.190 | 0.801 | 0.42 | mostly constant |
| 357 − 219 | −0.515 | 0.977 | **0.70** | not correctable |
| 219 − 218 | +2.260 | 0.720 | 0.24 | mostly constant |
| 357 − 019 | −0.660 | 1.224 | **1.02** | not correctable |

**BAO_0000357 — the chosen training format — is the one that diverges most.**
221 and 219 agree with each other after a constant shift; 357 against 219 and 019
does not. So it is not that COX-2 assays disagree in general. The format that
could not have been merged is the one being trained on, which makes the
single-format decision stronger rather than convenient.

### The prediction, and it held three times

Excess SD was computed from the paired deltas and used to **predict, in advance**,
which format-held-out splits would fail:

| transfer | excess SD | margin |
|---|---:|---:|
| 357 → 221 | 0.42 | **+0.086** |
| 357 → 219 | 0.70 | −0.013 |
| 357 → 019 | 1.02 | −0.187 |

Perfect inverse rank, Spearman −1.0, reproduced across three separate runs. Do
not quote a correlation coefficient on three points — the convincing part is that
the ordering was stated before the transfers were run, from a quantity computed
on different data.

### Scaffold concentration

```
25% of rows sit in the top  17 of 1,210 scaffolds
50% of rows sit in the top  83 of 1,210 scaffolds
80% of rows sit in the top 420 of 1,210 scaffolds
```

83 scaffolds hold half the data. The scaffold split is therefore genuinely hard,
and a random split would have flattered the model badly.

## Finding 2 — the potency model is modest but real

25 folds: 5 repeats × 5-fold scaffold CV, scaffold→fold assignment reshuffled per
repeat.

```
25 folds          mean +0.147   SD 0.044   range +0.073 to +0.231   all positive
reported          +0.141        RandomForest RMSE 0.989 vs null 1.130
per repeat        +0.147  +0.140  +0.163  +0.146  +0.139
between-repeat SD  0.0098
95% CI            +0.135 to +0.159
```

The partition barely matters — reshuffling which scaffolds land where moves the
answer by 0.01. The +0.141 is a property of the data, not of one lucky cut.

The gap between +0.147 and the reported +0.141 is the cost of *not* cherry-picking:
per-fold lines report the better of RandomForest and XGBoost, the aggregate fixes
on RandomForest throughout.

### Against the ceiling

Mean null 1.129. Inter-laboratory IC50 agreement 0.68 log units (Kalliokoski
2013, *PLoS ONE* 8:e61007), so a perfect model cannot score better than that.

```
best achievable margin = 1.129 − 0.68 = 0.449
achieved                                0.141   (33%)
```

**Unresolved**: whether 0.68 is the SD of the *difference between* two labs or the
per-measurement SD. If the former, per-measurement error is 0.68/√2 ≈ 0.48, the
ceiling becomes 0.649 and the fraction achieved falls to 23%. Read the paper and
settle it before quoting either.

### The other splits

| split | margin |
|---|---:|
| scaffold (5×5 CV) | **+0.141** |
| temporal | **−0.111** |
| format 357 → 221 | +0.086 |
| format 357 → 219 | −0.013 |
| format 357 → 019 | −0.187 |

**The temporal failure is a finding, not a defect.** Every real use is
prospective. A model that works under scaffold CV and fails when trained on older
papers and tested on newer ones would not have been useful prospectively, and
that is worth reporting.

Algorithm choice does not matter: across 25 folds RandomForest and XGBoost split
the wins roughly evenly, with margins within noise of each other. Two methods with
different inductive biases landing in the same place is evidence that the limit is
the data, not the model. Report RandomForest as primary and XGBoost as equivalent.

## Finding 3 — the classifier earns its place, on the strict test

Scaffold-split 5-fold, two definitions of "negative".

```
                                  all negatives    confirmed only
classifier: RandomForest          0.800 ± 0.014    0.845 ± 0.039
classifier: XGBoost               0.791 ± 0.025    0.838 ± 0.037
baseline: regressor, thresholded  0.777 ± 0.024    0.749 ± 0.073
null: majority class              0.500            0.500
null: heavy atoms                 0.474 ± 0.038    0.471 ± 0.038

verdict                           +0.023           +0.095
                                  no difference    classifier wins
```

**The direction of travel is the evidence.** Moving to the harder task, the
classifier improves by 0.045 and the regressor degrades by 0.028. That is the
pattern the mechanism predicts: the classifier trains on the 610 measured
inactives and the regressor structurally cannot, since ChEMBL records inactives as
`>` with no value to fit. The gap appears exactly where those inactives matter.

Class balance: all negatives 3,948 rows / 1,791 active (45%) — labels active
1,791, weak 1,547, inactive 610. Confirmed only 2,401 rows / 1,791 active (75%).
Both models given matched class weighting (`balanced`; `scale_pos_weight` 1.20 and
0.34 for XGBoost) so the comparison is between algorithms, not weighting schemes.

### EF1% — the screening number, with a trap

```
all negatives (45% active, ceiling 2.20)
  classifier         2.19      top 1% of the ranked list is ~99% active
  regressor          1.73
  random             1.00      top 1% would be 45% active

confirmed only (75% active, ceiling 1.34)
  everything         1.35      SATURATED — do not quote
```

On the all-negatives set the classifier reaches 2.19 against a mathematical
maximum of 2.20. On the confirmed set every row including both nulls reads 1.35,
because EF is capped by the base rate. **Report EF against the definitional random
value of 1.0**, not against the printed majority-class null — with constant
predictions `argsort` returns rows in file order, so that null's "top 1%" is the
first few rows rather than a random draw, which is why it reads 1.21 and 1.35
instead of 1.00.

### Interpreting Findings 2 and 3 together

The potency model captures a third of its available margin; the same model
thresholded classifies at 0.777. That is coherent, not contradictory. Labels carry
0.68 log units of noise, which swamps a continuous prediction but barely touches a
binary call at 1 µM — a 0.68 error only changes the answer for compounds near the
cutoff. The data supports a binary call better than a continuous one, and the
noise floor explains why.

**What the numbers support:** a binary prioritisation filter, within the chemical
space the model knows.
**What they do not support:** predicted IC50 values presented as usable, or any
claim of prospective performance, given the temporal failure.

## Finding 4 — a measured boundary on ligand-based transfer

CMFOA ethnopharmacological library. 5,821 records → **2,915 unique compounds**
after removing duplicates; 2,914 parsed, 1 failed.

```
PREDICTED ACTIVE (shortlist)        3    0.1%
predicted inactive                321   11.0%
REFUSED, outside domain         2,579   88.5%
REFUSED, unparseable                1    0.0%
EXCLUDED, already in training      11    0.4%
```

Applicability domain: `1 − mean Tanimoto to the 5 nearest training compounds`,
threshold at the 95th percentile of the training set's own neighbour distance =
**0.615**. Nothing chosen by hand.

### No threshold rescues it

```
query distance percentiles   5% 0.563   25% 0.684   50% 0.751   75% 0.799   95% 0.840
training set's own median    0.299

refused at 0.615  →  88.5%
refused at 0.700  →  69.7%
refused at 0.800  →  24.6%
refused at 0.900  →   0.3%
```

The median query sits at 0.751 — mean Tanimoto 0.25 to its five nearest training
compounds. Admitting most of the library needs a threshold near 0.85, which is
Tanimoto 0.15. Nothing is similar at 0.15. The refusal is not an artefact of a
strict cut-off, and the threshold at 0.615 was already permissive (Tanimoto 0.385,
below what most chemists would call similar).

### Two hypotheses tested and rejected

**H1: glycosylation and molecular size explain the gap. REJECTED.**

```
                  n      median MW    ≥1 sugar-like ring
IN DOMAIN       334         341             16%
REFUSED       2,580         302             17%
```

Equivalent, and the refused compounds are if anything lighter. Aglycone
preprocessing would not have helped. *(Sugar rings counted by a loose proxy: a
6-membered ring with exactly one ring oxygen.)*

**H2: the library lacks stereochemistry, so 3D methods are infeasible. REJECTED.**

```
TRAINING   4,540 parsed | fully specified stereo 3,606 (79%) | too big for PubChem 3D   40 (1%)
CMFOA      2,914 parsed | fully specified stereo 2,388 (82%) | too big for PubChem 3D  192 (7%)
```

CMFOA is *better* specified than the training set. This was predicted from three
pasted structures which turned out to be the library's worst cases — saponins with
17 and 28 unspecified centres (2¹⁷ and 2.7×10⁸ stereoisomers). At population level
the concern does not hold, and 3D methods are viable for 82% of the library.

### What the gap is

Not size, not decoration, not annotation. **Chemotype.** The training set is
synthetic medicinal chemistry — diaryl heterocycles, sulfonamides, arylacetic
acids. CMFOA is terpenoids, alkaloids, phenylpropanoids, polyketides. Different
ring systems. No preprocessing addresses that, because there is nothing to strip
or normalise; the molecules are built differently.

### The claim

> A COX-2 model trained on ChEMBL enzyme-assay data (n=3,338; scaffold-split
> margin +0.141, 95% CI +0.135 to +0.159; classifier AUC 0.845 against confirmed
> inactives) cannot assess 88.5% of a 2,915-compound ethnopharmacological library.
> Median applicability-domain distance for the library is 0.751 against 0.299 for
> the training set itself, and no threshold between 0.6 and 0.9 yields defensible
> coverage. The gap is not explained by molecular weight or glycosylation, which
> are equivalent in the accepted and refused sets, nor by missing stereochemical
> annotation, which is *more* complete in the library than in the training data.
> It is attributable to chemotype.

The applicability domain is the instrument that measured this, the criterion came
from the training data rather than from a choice that produced a convenient
answer, and the two obvious confounders were tested and excluded. Three hits out
of 2,915 would not have been a result. This is.

**No enrichment test was run.** The library carries traditional-use annotations
(`Potential Traditional Uses`, semicolon-separated multi-label, vocabulary
includes Industrial, Narcotic, Tonic, Emolient, Bactericidal, Skin) but only 334
compounds survived the domain gate, too few for the comparison to mean anything.
The positive-class term must be chosen and recorded **before** predictions are
seen (§9b item 2).

## Code state

Two commands plus a screen. Everything else lives in per-target JSON config.

```bash
python pipeline.py census --config targets/cox2.json      # fetch + count, then STOP
python pipeline.py build  --config targets/cox2.json --repeats 5
python pipeline.py screen --config targets/cox2.json --input list.csv \
    --smiles-col SMILES --id-col ID --exclude-known
```

| flag | effect |
|---|---|
| `--repeats N` | repeats scaffold CV with the scaffold→fold assignment reshuffled. Default 1 measures one arbitrary partition and understates uncertainty. Use 5 for anything reported |
| `--blocks {both,fingerprint,descriptors}` | feature-block ablation. Outputs suffixed so runs cannot overwrite each other; nulls held fixed so margins stay comparable |
| `--splits scaffold,temporal,format` | restrict split axes. For iteration only — outputs suffixed so a partial run cannot be mistaken for the real table |
| `--score-all` | scores out-of-domain compounds instead of refusing them, marked `OUT OF DOMAIN`, never entering the shortlist |
| `--ad-percentile N` | moves the domain threshold |
| `--exclude-known` | drops compounds already in training from the shortlist and enrichment test |
| `--gate regressor` | uses the thresholded potency model as the gate |

The screen now **caches the fitted model** to `models/`, keyed on a hash of the
config fields that matter plus the feature files' size and mtime. Verified to
invalidate on a config edit, a feature rebuild, and a `--blocks` change. Cold run
15s, warm 3s on synthetic data; the saving on the real set is larger. Query
featurisation is parallel across cores and prediction is one batched call.

Sanity checks done: the vectorised Tanimoto matches RDKit's `BulkTanimotoSimilarity`
to 5×10⁻⁸; the AD self-exclusion drops the perfect self-match; `_random_grouped_folds`
gives distinct, leak-free partitions per seed (sklearn's `GroupKFold` is
deterministic and ignores `random_state`, so renaming groups returns the same
partition — the fix randomises the *visiting order* instead).

### The pharmacophore experiment — three arms, not two

Motivated by Finding 4: a pharmacophore representation abstracts away the
scaffold, so the applicability domain computed under it may admit compounds that
ECFP4 refuses. That is the testable claim.

Identical Gobbi pharmacophore feature definitions and signature factory
throughout; only the distance source changes. `Gen2DFingerprint(mol, factory,
dMat=Get3DDistanceMatrix(mol))` gives the 3D version with nothing else varying —
verified working, and the two differ substantially (Tanimoto 0.244 on aspirin).

| arm | distances | needs conformers | needs stereo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2D** | topological | no | no |
| **single-conformer 3D** | Euclidean, one ETKDGv3 conformer | yes | yes |
| **ensemble 3D** | Euclidean, union over 10–50 conformers | yes, ×10–50 | yes |

The third arm exists because a pharmacophore is conformation-dependent. A
single-conformer 3D fingerprint is noisy in a way the 2D one is not — 2D is
conformation-independent by construction. **If single-conformer 3D loses to 2D,
that is the most useful of the three results, because it is what most people would
actually have done.**

Measured against ECFP4 as baseline, on the same splits and nulls:

- **refusal rate on CMFOA** under each representation — does the domain widen?
- **margin over null** (regression) and **AUC / EF1%** (classification) on the
  scaffold split, on the subset where all arms are computable
- **coverage** reported separately from performance

**The trap to avoid.** A lower refusal rate is only good if the model performs on
the compounds it newly admits. Changing representation could simply produce a more
permissive gate with no more predictive power. Both numbers are required, or the
experiment fools you — which is the failure mode the rest of this methodology
exists to prevent.

Embedding cost measured: 0.02s for a rigid drug-like molecule, 0.61s for a
126-atom saponin, plus MMFF optimisation. ~6,000 molecules parallelised is under
an hour for the single-conformer arm.

### Facts established today that are worth keeping

- ChEMBL provides **no 3D coordinates**. Its utilities generate 2D only
  (Schrodinger coordgen); the `is3D` endpoint merely checks whether coordinates
  are already present.
- PubChem has 3D for ~89% of its compounds (OpenEye OMEGA) but excludes >50
  non-hydrogen atoms, >15 rotatable bonds, non-organic elements and multi-unit
  structures — which excludes 7% of CMFOA, concentrated in exactly the saponins
  and larger glycosides. Mixing OMEGA conformers with self-generated ones would
  confound the comparison anyway.
- **Generate conformers yourself.** It is the only route that is consistent across
  both the training set and the query library.
